# Hack2Skill submission — copy/paste

## Challenge
Real-Time AI for Critical Decisions

## What is the Problem you are trying to solve?
SSF2026. Indians lost Rs 22,845 crore to cyber fraud in 2024 (+206%), and "digital arrest" and fake-KYC scams are growing fast. A victim on a fake police/bank call sends money over UPI in seconds; the receiving mule account forwards it down a chain within minutes and cashes out, so recovery is rare. Today's defences fail: rules like "large amount to a new payee" catch under a third of scams while blocking honest users, per-transaction models see one payment in isolation, and complaint portals act after settlement. Each signal alone (a call, a payment, an account receiving money) looks innocent. Banks need a decision on every payment, before the money settles, that also sees the scam call and the mule network behind it, without slowing UPI down or annoying genuine users.

## How are you planning to solve the problem? (Brief Overview)
Kavach scores every UPI payment inside the transaction and returns ALLOW, STEP-UP (OTP + cooling-off with a scam warning) or HOLD (block, alert victim), with plain-language reasons. It fuses 3 signals: (1) the payment vs the sender's habits (amount, new payee, new device, bursts, odd hour); (2) the money flow: mule-graph features such as receiver fan-in from first-time payers and pass-through (money out minutes after money in), plus a mule watchlist that freezes repeat receivers; (3) a Hinglish scam-call classifier whose risk attaches to the user for 2 hours. A streaming feature store (same code for training and live scoring, no leakage) feeds IBM Snap ML boosted trees. FastAPI + WebSocket dashboard and REST API. Trained and run on IBM LinuxONE (z17): on held-out days, at 0.1% false alarms it catches 97.3% of frauds vs 79.6% for a per-transaction model and 28% for rules (PR-AUC 0.992); live replay stops 98.4% of fraud payments while holding 0.046% of legit ones; ~28 µs per payment.

## How does your solution leverage IBM Z / LinuxONE technologies?
Kavach is trained, tested and served on an IBM LinuxONE Community Cloud VM running on an IBM z17 (s390x). Inference uses IBM Snap ML: the model is exported to PMML and re-imported with tree_format="auto"; with IBM's zDNN library installed, Snap ML compiles the trees to zDNN tensors and runs them on the Telum on-chip AI accelerator (NNPA), the same chip as the payment workload, so every payment is scored inside the transaction with no hop to an AI server. We benchmarked both z17 paths: the accelerator gives identical decisions on all 33,538 test payments (49 µs p50); the z17 cores run our compact model in 28 µs (~285k payments/s in batches of 64). Payment and call data never leave the platform: no external AI APIs, model integrity pinned by SHA-256, audit log of every decision. IBM Z already runs most of the world's card and bank transactions, so Kavach fits where UPI switches and bank cores already live.
